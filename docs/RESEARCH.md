# Research notes (market scan)

**Scan date:** October 29, 2026. Market features/prices change; this is a qualitative design scan, not a controlled product evaluation. The cited comparisons were read for their descriptions of target audience and instruction style. Claims about comparative learning outcomes are not inferred from app-store ratings or vendor marketing.

## Existing products reviewed

| App | What it offers (from the reviewed source) | Design note for Word Garden |
|---|---|---|
| Khan Academy Kids | Broad early-learning suite, including reading/phonics, stories, math and creative activities; described as free and ad-free by Khan Academy | A helpful broad free comparator; Word Garden narrows scope to sustained decoding and encoding practice instead of a broad app catalogue. |
| Duolingo ABC | Early-literacy lessons for preschool through early elementary, including letters, phonics and foundational reading; Common Sense review calls out simple drills | Keep learning prompts short and explicit; do not copy its branded mechanics or claim its outcomes. |
| Teach Your Monster to Read | Story/adventure framing wrapped around a systematic phonics progression; phonics.org review notes letter sounds, blending, and optional collectible customization | Keep a clear skill progression, but let the literacy action—not story advancement or collecting—occupy most of the session. |
| Phonics Hero | Synthetic phonics practice from letter sounds through blending, spelling and sentences; described by EducationalAppStore as highly game-themed | Include encoding, not just choosing a letter; use a quiet non-competitive wrapper. |
| Reading Eggs | Stepwise lessons, phonics and e-books with progress tracking (as described by EducationalAppStore) | Avoid overloading a first version with a large library; prioritize a transparent sequence. |
| ABCmouse | Broad subscription early-learning curriculum; phonics is one strand (as summarized in app comparisons) | Stay narrowly focused on reading/spelling. |
| Starfall | Phonics activities, songs and games; commonly used as a supplement (as described in app comparisons) | Offer direct sound-letter, blending and spelling practice in one short session. |

### Market implications

The recurring instructional distinction is letter recognition vs. being able to blend phonemes to decode, and recognizing a printed word vs. encoding it from sounds. Word Garden therefore has distinct listen-for-a-sound, blend-to-a-picture, and build-the-spelling tasks. Sentence prompts are a small transfer activity, not an open-ended story library. Sessions stay short, predictable and low-stimulation; the garden grows only after the child completes a session, with no coins, streaks, timer, leaderboard, ads or purchase prompts. Progress is local and reported in plain language.

## Instructional rationale

The National Reading Panel's historical synthesis defines phonics as teaching letter-sound relations and their use in reading and spelling. It reports systematic phonics instruction benefited word reading and spelling in early grades, and that explicit phonemic-awareness instruction that teaches children to manipulate phonemes with letters can support reading and spelling. The report also cautions that phonemic-awareness work is one part of a full reading program, not a complete program by itself. Word Garden translates that into sequential letter-sound practice, sound blending, spelling from phonemes, and decodable word/sentence examples. Its content has **not** been validated by a reading specialist and should be reviewed before classroom or clinical use.
